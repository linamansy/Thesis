You are assisting with business process modelling. Split a rule from a legal text into its parts.

INSTRUCTIONS
- "duty_type": what the rule does:
  - "duty": it places a duty on someone (something must or must not be done).
  - "right": it gives someone a right or an entitlement (for example to ask for something, to withdraw or to be paid).
  - "none": it places no duty and gives no right of its own; it only limits, excludes or refers to other rules (for example "paragraphs 1 to 3 shall not apply where ..." or "without prejudice to ..."). For "none", give "roles": ["other"], "if": [], "then": "", "exceptions": [] and "special_cases": [].
- "roles": who the rule places its duty on, using only these names:
{role_lines}
  If the duty is on more than one of them (for example "{pair_example}"), list each.
- "if": EVERY situational condition of the rule: the kind of situation or activity it is about, the event that triggers it, and conditions on the data or the people (for example a clause starting with "where", "when" or "if"). Write each as a short statement in the words of the rule. Never put a role into "if"; roles go into "roles". Use an empty list only if the rule names no situation at all and simply places a duty on someone.
- "if" is a list of groups, and every group must hold. Each group has "logic" and "items":
  - "all": every item of the group must hold (conditions joined by "and", or a single condition).
  - "any": the items are alternatives and at least one of them must hold (for example "any of", "one of the following", or conditions joined by "or"). Put all alternatives of the same choice into one "any" group.
- Each item has "text" (the condition) and "kind":
  - "process_fact": a fact that holds or does not hold for the process as a whole (for example the kind of activity, of customer or of goods). What is sold, the type of contract, of content or of service, and who the parties are are ALWAYS process facts.
  - "case_event": ONLY something an actor does during a single case of the process: requests, withdraws, pays, delivers, returns, complains or cancels. Nothing else is a case event.
- A condition says WHEN the rule applies, never what someone has to do. Never put an obligation into "if": an "if" item must not contain the words "must", "shall" or "is required to"; such an answer is rejected automatically.
- "then": what the rule requires to be done when it applies, in the words of the rule, with its timing and every trigger it states (for example "at the time when", "on request", "where applicable").
- "exceptions": the situations the rule itself names in which it does not apply (for example "unless ..." or "this does not apply where ..."), each as a short statement of that situation. Use an empty list if the rule names none.
- "special_cases": a clause that starts a special case with a different duty (for example "In the case of ...", or "Where ..." followed by a duty that differs from the main duty) is its own rule. Give each such clause with "clause" (its first words, copied from the rule), "roles", "if" and "then", in the same format as above. Its conditions do not go into the main "if", and its duty does not go into the main "then". Use an empty list if the rule has no such clause.
- Use only the legal passage below.
- Answer with one JSON object that follows the JSON schema below. Output only the JSON, with no other text.
